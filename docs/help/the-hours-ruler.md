# The hours ruler

Pick up a task and drop it on the clock. The hours of your day open in a card under the **Next** line, and the task gets the time you drop it on.

## Drag a task onto the hours

1. Press on a task's words and start dragging it. The board dims a little, and today's hours open under the **Next** line, from now on, with "now" marked and your other timed tasks as dots.
2. Move along the hours. Under the slip, the time it would get: 14:00, 14:15, 14:30…
3. Drop it on an hour. The task shows its new time, and the ruler folds back.

- It snaps to the quarter hour: "snaps to 15 min · Shift for exact". Hold **Shift** for the exact minute.
- Drop it on **Tomorrow**, at the end of the ruler, for the start of tomorrow's hours, or hold over it to see them. **Back to today** returns.
- **Esc** while carrying sends the task back where it was.
- A line from the Inbox strip can go straight onto the hours too.

The hours shown run from now to the end of your day (**Settings › My day**, 08:00 to 22:00 at first). A task in **Work**, with **Settings › Work hours** on, goes to a little after your work hours end (the next morning, for hours that cross midnight), and its **Tomorrow** is the next work day (by its name when that isn't tomorrow). The hours read "14", or "2 PM" when Windows uses a 12-hour clock. Late in the evening, with less than half an hour left, the ruler shows tomorrow's hours at once.

## Without dragging

Open the task's menu (**⋯**) › **Pick on today's hours**, then click an hour. **Close the hours** leaves it as it was.

From the keyboard, the hours take the keys as they open: **←** / **→** move by 15 minutes (with **Shift**, by an hour), **Home** / **End** go to the first and last hour, **Enter** sets the time. **Tab** goes on to **Tomorrow** (**Enter** picks it) and **Esc** closes.

## The Next line

When nothing is being carried, the **Next** line shows your next timed tasks, so what's coming is always in sight.
